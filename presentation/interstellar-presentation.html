Mission Motivation

Sci-fi shouldn't need a second screen

You're watching Interstellar. Cooper just delivered the message from inside the tesseract. You have a dozen questions- and your only option is to pause the film, open a browser, and hope a search result or a generic chatbot gets it right.

The Viewer's Problem

  • Great sci-fi rewards curiosity- but the moment a question hits, viewers have no grounded way to ask it without leaving the film.
  • Vanilla LLMs answer fluently even when they don't know, and can't distinguish a fan theory from a line actually in the script.
  • Fan wikis and explainer channels are the closest substitute today- unofficial, uneven in quality, and outside the filmmaker's control.

The Creator's Problem

  • Studios and filmmakers have no easy, official way to help audiences go deeper on plot, science, or meaning.
  • Every question a confused viewer can't get answered is a viewer who disengages, or who goes looking for answers somewhere the studio doesn't control.
  • There's currently no product that lets a film's own creators stand behind an accurate, on-brand companion for their movie.

Why Interstellar is our proving ground

  • Narrative source- the screenplay provides dense, structured plot and dialogue.
  • Scientific source- "The Science of Interstellar" gives an authoritative reference for black holes, relativity, and wormholes covered in the film.
  • Opinion source- YouTube comments are noisy, contradictory, and emotionally charged, stress-testing retrieval and fusion against real-world conversational data.

THE BET WE'RE MAKING

Every great sci-fi film already has an audience asking these exact questions, at scale, in the comments section, every single day. We think that audience deserves a better answer than a search engine- and that films deserve a companion that actually knows the material. Interstellar is proof it works for one movie. The tesseract scene in Interstellar is a creative amalgamation of theoretical physics and narrative invention, with its scientific accuracy debated across differ ent perspectives. From a physics standpoint, the tesseract is portrayed as a 5-dimensional hypercube enabling Cooper to transcend time by existing in a higher-dime nsional space. While this draws on concepts like string theory's extra dimensions and Einstein's relativity (which treats time as a dimension), the film's depictio n of a navigable, physical tesseract is not grounded in real science. Current physics suggests higher dimensions, if they exist, are compactified or hidden, not tr aversable spaces. The scene's mechanics-such as Cooper manipulating the tesseract's faces or transmitting messages via gravity-remain speculative, blending quantum entanglement and time dilation into a narrative device rather than rigorous scientific explanation.

Fans' reactions reflect this tension between ambition and approximation. Many praise the film's effort to merge hard science with storytelling, especially the visu ally stunning tesseract set (which used real practical effects) and its role in resolving the plot's emotional core. However, critics and scientists note simplific ations or fictionalized elements, such as the tesseract's structure or the physics of higher-dimensional interaction. Some fans humorously dismiss it as overly sim plified ("Einstein would laugh"), while others celebrate its imaginative leap as a cultural icon of science-fantasy. Overall, the tesseract is seen as a thought-pr ovoking, if imperfect, bridge between scientific theory and artistic storytelling, with its legacy rooted in sparking curiosity about physics and its limits.

Flight Log

Challenges faced

Each issue below includes the underlying cause and where we mitigated it - measured numbers are marked as placeholders for the team to fill in.

Multi-source embedding mismatch

Script, textbook, and comment text differ sharply in length, register, and noise level, so a single embedding space underperforms on at least one source.

Noisy YouTube comment data

Sarcasm, off-topic replies, and duplicate bot comments dilute the relevant signal in the comments corpus.

Retrieval latency

Multi-hop and HyDE retrieval add sequential LLM calls before generation can begin.

Hardware constraints

Running Qwen3:8B and embedding models locally via Ollama is bound by available GPU memory.

Mitigation strategies

  • Chunk size and overlap tuning per source type.
  • Hybrid (dense + sparse) retrieval to recover keyword-exact matches missed by pure semantic search.
  • Caching of repeated or near-duplicate queries.

Next Trajectory

From One movie to EVERY movie

Today, this is a single-film proof of concept, built for Interstellar. Here's where we think it goes next.

Phase 1- Today: one film, proven end-to-end

A working multi-agent companion for a single movie, grounded in its script, its science, and its real audience- showing the architecture, the routing, and the answer quality all hold up on a real title.

Phase 2 - Near-term: A Companion per film

  • Creators of sci-fi (and eventually any) movies come to us to build a dedicated companion bot for their own film.
  • Each film gets its own hosted destination- in the spirit of a dedicated site like interstellar.com- where any viewer can search up doubts or questions about the movie.
  • No player integration required. The film gets a stickier, more discoverable presence online, and a self-serve way to help its audience go deeper.

Phase 3 - Long-term: Embedded in the movie player

  • The same companion, live inside the video player itself- ask a question the moment it occurs to you, mid-scene, without pausing or leaving the film.
  • Requires platform-level integration, so this is the long-range goal rather than the next release- Phase 2 is the realistic path to get there.

Technical roadmap alongside the product roadmap

  • Generalize the pipeline- parameterize ingestion, chunking, and indexing so a new film's script, companion material, and comment corpus can be swapped in without rearchitecting the core stages.
  • Better cross-source alignment- a shared or jointly-trained embedding space across script, textbook, and comments.
  • Improved reranking models- a learned reranker placed after initial retrieval to sharpen final context selection.
  • Real-time comment ingestion- move from a static CSV snapshot to a live-updating comment stream.
  • Better agent collaboration strategies- richer negotiation between agents beyond the current Supervisor + Fusion pattern.
  • Multimodal expansion- incorporate frames and stills from the film alongside text sources.
